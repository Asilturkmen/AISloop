# Preferences

The taste profile that AISloop's rules follow. Rules are derived from this file, never the other way round. When a rule and this file disagree, this file wins, and the rule gets fixed.

Each entry says where it came from:
- **voted:** chosen on a review board (link to the decision in `decisions.md`)
- **stated:** said directly by the owner
- **observed:** inferred from a past project's accepted or rejected designs (lower confidence; confirm on a board)

## Direction

- Interfaces should feel like Apple's own **app** software (Settings, Notes, Reminders, Mail, Finder), not like apple.com marketing pages. *(stated)*
- Calm, clear and carefully made. Nothing should look generated. *(stated)*

## Observed in past projects (to be confirmed)

- Options must differ in structure, not just in styling. Three variations of the same skeleton were rejected. *(observed)*
- In each design round, change one thing and say what stays the same. Changing two things radically at once was rejected as too aggressive. *(observed)*
- A list stays aligned like a list, even inside a chat-style layout. Free-floating centred lines were rejected; items aligned to columns were accepted. *(observed)*
- Text is never cut off. Alignment and consistent spacing matter more than decoration. *(observed)*
- The thing a user acts on should look like an object (a surface with a clear shape and hit area), not a bare coloured text link. *(observed)*
- One calm accent colour. *(observed)*

## Open questions for the first review board

- Which side of Apple's language matters most: typography, whitespace, materials (translucency), density, or motion?
- How dense? Airy like iOS, or compact like macOS?
- Where, if anywhere, is translucent "glass" welcome?
- Large bold titles or quiet small headers?
- Icons in lists and menus: yes or no?
